Starting in the gym is the hardest part, and the first few months are also when progress comes fastest.
You do not need a complicated plan, expensive supplements or a perfect diet.
You need a simple programme, good technique, a little patience and a record of what you lifted.

## Before you start

- **See a doctor first** if you have heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, a past injury, or are pregnant.
- **Get your numbers.** Use the [TDEE calculator](/calculators/tdee/) for calories and the [protein calculator](/calculators/protein/) for protein. [Calories and TDEE](/guides/calories-and-tdee/) explains the method.
- **Pick a gym or a space at home** you can reach easily. Convenience beats a fancier gym you rarely visit.
- **Wear comfortable clothes and flat, stable shoes.** Running shoes with thick, soft soles are not ideal for squats and deadlifts.

## The first 12 weeks

Train **3 days a week on a full-body programme**, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday.
Each muscle gets worked about three times a week, which is ideal for learning movements.
The [tracker](/app/) has ready-made full-body programmes for a gym, dumbbells only, or bodyweight at home.

### Weeks 1-4: learn the movements

- Do **2-3 sets** per exercise.
- Stop every set with **2-3 reps left in reserve.**
- Focus on technique and showing up. You are building a habit as much as a body.
- Total volume: about **6-10 hard sets per muscle per week**, the right range for a beginner.

### Weeks 5-8: start pushing

- Most exercises go to **3 sets.**
- Take compound lifts to **1-3 reps in reserve** and isolation exercises to **0-1.**
- Add weight or reps almost every session using double progression (below).

### Weeks 9-12: keep progressing

- Keep adding load. Most beginners can still progress nearly every week at this stage.
- Take an **easy week after about 6 weeks** of hard training, or sooner if lifts stall: about 40% fewer sets at similar loads.
- At the end of week 12, compare your lifts, waist and photos with week 1.

After 12 weeks you can stay on full body, which works for years, or move to a 4-day upper/lower split.
See [how to build muscle](/guides/how-to-build-muscle/).

## Technique basics

Good technique lets you train harder for longer without getting hurt.

- **Control the lowering.** Take about 2-3 seconds to lower the weight.
- **Use a full range of motion** that you can control.
- **Keep a braced, neutral spine** on squats, deadlifts and rows. Take a breath into your belly and tighten your core before each rep.
- **Keep the same form on every rep.** If the last reps look different from the first, the set is over.
- **Never bounce** weights off your chest or the floor.
- **Warm up** with 1-3 lighter sets before your first heavy exercise.

Ways to learn:

- Record your sets on your phone from the side and compare with a reliable demonstration.
- Ask a qualified trainer to check your form on the main lifts.
- Start compound lifts with lighter variations: goblet squats before barbell squats, dumbbell Romanian deadlifts before barbell ones.

## How much to lift

### Your first session

For each exercise, pick a weight you can lift for the **bottom of the rep range with 2-3 reps to spare.**
If the range is 8-12, choose a weight you could lift about 10-11 times, and do 8.
Too light is fine for the first week; too heavy is not.

### Double progression

1. Keep the same weight and add reps each session.
2. When every set reaches the top of the range, add the smallest weight step next time.
3. Drop back to the bottom of the range and build up again.

For example, on an 8-12 range: 30 kg for 8, 8, 8, then 9, 9, 8, and so on until 12, 12, 12; then 32.5 kg for 8, 8, 8.

The [tracker](/app/) tells you the target for every set.
You can estimate your one-rep max at any time with the [one-rep max calculator](/calculators/one-rep-max/); there is no need to test it as a beginner.

### Rest between sets

- 2-3 minutes for compound lifts
- 60-90 seconds for isolation exercises

## Soreness

Muscle soreness that appears a day or two after training is normal, especially in the first weeks and after new exercises.

- It usually peaks 1-3 days after a session and fades on its own.
- It gets much milder after the first few weeks.
- **It is not a measure of a good workout.** Progress in your log is.
- Light movement, walking, sleep and enough protein help more than anything you can buy.
- If you are very sore, train anyway with about 25% fewer sets. See [training when sick or tired](/guides/training-when-sick-or-tired/).

**Sharp pain, joint pain or pain that does not ease is not normal.**
Stop that exercise and get it checked if it does not settle.

## Eating as a beginner

- **Protein** is the priority: about 1.6-2.2 g per kg depending on your goal. See [macros explained](/guides/macros-explained/).
- **Beginners can often build muscle while losing fat,** especially if they start with some extra body fat. Eating at maintenance with high protein works well for this.
- **Underweight or very lean?** Eat in a small surplus. See [lean bulk](/guides/lean-bulk/).
- Use everyday Indian foods: dal, curd, paneer, eggs, chicken, fish, soya, chana. The [Indian diet for fitness](/guides/indian-diet-for-fitness/) guide has sample days.

## What to track

- **Body weight:** every morning, using the weekly average
- **Lifts:** weight, reps and sets for each exercise
- **Waist and photos:** every 2-4 weeks
- **Food:** calories and protein, at least for the first month
- **Sleep and energy**

Change only one thing at a time, and give each change about two weeks.

## Common mistakes

- **Doing too much too soon.** Five days of hard training in week one leads to burnout or injury.
- **Programme hopping.** Changing your plan every two weeks means you never progress on anything.
- **Lifting too heavy with poor form** to keep up with others in the gym.
- **Lifting too light for too long.** By week 5, sets should be genuinely hard.
- **Not logging.** Memory is unreliable, and progress hides without numbers.
- **Skipping legs.**
- **Not eating enough protein,** or dieting too hard while trying to build muscle.
- **Spending on supplements before fixing basics.** See [supplements](/guides/supplements/).
- **Comparing yourself with social media.** Lighting, angles and sometimes drugs are involved.

## The mindset that works

Consistency beats intensity.
Three sessions a week for a year will transform your body far more than six sessions a week for a month.
Expect slow weeks, missed sessions and plateaus, and keep going anyway.

This is general information, not medical advice; see a doctor before starting if you have a medical condition, an injury, or are pregnant.
