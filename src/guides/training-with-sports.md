Lifting makes almost every athlete better: stronger, more powerful and more resistant to injury.
The challenge is fitting it around a sport without one wrecking the other.
These are the principles that make the two work together.

## Decide what comes first

Your priority decides how everything else is arranged.

| Main goal | How to lift |
| --- | --- |
| The sport | 2-3 full-body sessions a week, 6-12 hard sets per muscle, built to support the sport |
| Building muscle | Treat the sport as extra activity and schedule lifting around it |

Being honest about this avoids the common trap of trying to train like a bodybuilder and an athlete at the same time, and recovering like neither.

## The interference effect

Large amounts of endurance work can blunt strength and muscle gain.
This matters most with long, frequent running or cycling sessions, and much less with a few hours of a stop-start sport like badminton or football.

To keep it small:

- **Keep endurance volume manageable.** More is not automatically better.
- **Use lower-impact options** such as cycling or swimming for extra conditioning, rather than more running.
- **Do the more important session first** on any day with both.
- **Separate them** as much as your schedule allows.

## Scheduling

### Same day or separate days

- Put lifting and sport on **different days** where possible.
- If they must share a day, separate them by **about 6 hours or more**, for example lifting in the morning and practice in the evening.
- If they are back to back, do the higher-priority one first.

### Protect key days

- **No heavy leg training the day before a match, a long run or a key practice.**
- Hard leg days are best placed right after a match or long session, so you have the most time to recover before the next one.
- Do not stack heavy legs on consecutive days with intense running or jumping.

### A sample week for a weekend sport

| Day | Session |
| --- | --- |
| Mon | Full-body lift, heavier lower body |
| Tue | Sport practice |
| Wed | Full-body lift, upper-body focus, light legs |
| Thu | Sport practice |
| Fri | Rest or a short, light lift |
| Sat | Match |
| Sun | Rest, walking, mobility |

Cricket players with long match days, footballers with two practices a week and runners with a weekly long run can all use this shape, moving the lifts to suit the sport days.

## What to lift

For most sports, a short list of compound exercises covers the bases:

- A squat or lunge pattern
- A hinge, such as a Romanian deadlift or hip thrust
- An upper-body push and pull
- Single-leg work for balance and knee health
- Core work that resists rotation, plus some direct neck, calf and grip work where the sport needs it

Train with good technique and leave 1-3 reps in reserve on compounds, so lifting adds strength without leaving you too sore to play.
Use double progression to build load steadily; see [how to build muscle](/guides/how-to-build-muscle/).

## Eating for both

Sport burns a lot of energy, and under-eating is the most common reason players lose muscle or stall.

### Calories

- Start from your TDEE with an activity factor that reflects your whole week, often **very active (1.725)** or **extremely active (1.9)** for players training most days. Use the [TDEE calculator](/calculators/tdee/).
- Intense play typically burns roughly **300-600 kcal an hour**, depending on the sport and your size. The [calories-burned calculator](/calculators/calories-burned/) gives an estimate.
- If you want to gain muscle, the surplus goes on top of that.
- After 2-4 weeks of logs, the [tracker](/app/) estimates your real maintenance from your intake and weight trend, which is far more accurate than any estimate.

### Carbs

Use **more carbohydrate on sport days** and around hard sessions.
Rice, roti, poha, idli, bananas and dates are easy, cheap options.
See [meal timing](/guides/meal-timing/) for what to eat before, during and after.

### Protein

Keep protein at your goal's target every day: 1.8 g per kg when gaining, 1.6 g when maintaining, or 2.2 g on a cut.
The [protein calculator](/calculators/protein/) works this out.

### Fluids

Long matches in Indian heat cause heavy sweat losses.
Drink well before play, sip water through it, and add electrolytes for long or very hot sessions: nimbu pani with salt, coconut water or ORS.
Weighing yourself before and after a session shows how much fluid you lost; drink back a little more than that over the next few hours.

## In-season training

When matches come every week, the goal shifts from building to keeping.

- **1-2 lifting sessions a week** are enough to hold most of your strength and muscle.
- **Cut volume, keep load.** Fewer sets, but keep the weights fairly heavy.
- **Trim volume further in competition weeks,** and skip lifting entirely in the days just before an important match.
- **Avoid new exercises in-season;** unfamiliar movements cause the most soreness.

### Off-season

This is the time to build.
Lift 3-4 times a week, push volume up gradually, and accept some temporary soreness and fatigue.

## Recovery is non-negotiable

- **Sleep 7-9 hours,** more during heavy blocks.
- **Hit fluids and protein every day.**
- **Plan deloads** around tournaments or after heavy blocks of sport, with about 40% fewer lifting sets at similar loads.
- **Watch for warning signs:** falling performance, persistent soreness, poor sleep, low motivation or joint pain.

See [sleep and recovery](/guides/sleep-and-recovery/) for more.

This is general information, not medical advice; see a doctor or sports physiotherapist about injuries, pain or a return to sport after injury.
