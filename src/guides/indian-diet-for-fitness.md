You do not need imported foods or a special diet to train well in India.
Dal, roti, rice, sabzi and curd are a solid base; most people just need more protein on the plate and a little less oil.
This guide shows how to do that for every kind of eater.

## Start with the plate

ICMR-NIN's Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024) describe a balanced plate built mostly from vegetables, fruit, whole grains and pulses, with milk or curd, some nuts and seeds, and limited oil, salt and sugar.
That is a good template.
For someone who trains, the main change is to make sure every meal has a clear protein source.

Combining cereals and pulses, as in dal-chawal, rajma-rice or khichdi, also improves the overall quality of plant protein.

## Protein sources and portions

Values are approximate, for the amount as bought (raw or dry) unless stated.

### Vegetarian

| Food | Portion | Protein | kcal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soya chunks (dry) | 50 g | 26 g | 165 |
| Hung curd or Greek yoghurt | 200 g | 20 g | 145 |
| Paneer | 100 g | 18 g | 300 |
| Low-fat cottage cheese | 100 g | 10 g | 80 |
| Besan | 50 g (2 chillas) | 11 g | 195 |
| Rajma or chana (dry) | 50 g | 10-11 g | 170-190 |
| Toned milk | 250 ml | 8 g | 125 |
| Peanuts | 30 g | 7 g | 175 |
| Curd | 200 g | 7 g | 120 |
| Toor, moong or masoor dal (dry) | 30 g (1 katori cooked) | 6-7 g | 105 |
| Roti | 1 (about 40 g atta) | 5 g | 135 |

Paneer is a good protein source but carries a lot of fat, so keep portions measured on a cut.

### Eggetarian additions

| Food | Portion | Protein | kcal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whole egg | 1 large | 6 g | 72 |
| Egg white | 1 | 3.5 g | 17 |

### Non-vegetarian additions

| Food | Portion (raw) | Protein | kcal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canned tuna | 1 can (120 g) | 23 g | 105 |
| Chicken breast | 100 g | 22 g | 120 |
| Mutton, lean | 100 g | 20 g | 110 |
| Prawns | 100 g | 20 g | 85 |
| Rohu or other carp | 100 g | 18 g | 125 |

Chicken curry cut with skin and bone gives less protein per 100 g than boneless breast, so weigh the meat, not the bones.

### Vegan

Soya chunks, tofu, dals, chana, rajma, besan, peanuts and seeds carry the load.

| Food | Portion | Protein | kcal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tofu (firm) | 100 g | 17 g | 145 |
| Soy milk (unsweetened) | 250 ml | 7 g | 80 |
| Pumpkin seeds | 10 g (1 tbsp) | 3 g | 55 |

Vegans need a reliable source of vitamin B12 and should watch calcium; see [supplements](/guides/supplements/).

## Sample days at about 2,200 kcal

These are templates, not prescriptions.
Each is roughly 2,200 kcal with 110-135 g of protein, suitable for a 60-70 kg person cutting or a smaller person maintaining.
Oil for the whole day is 15-20 g (3-4 teaspoons), measured.

### Vegetarian (about 2,200 kcal, 120 g protein)

- **Breakfast:** 2 besan chillas (60 g besan) with spinach, and 200 g hung curd
- **Mid-morning:** 40 g oats cooked in 250 ml toned milk, with a banana
- **Lunch:** 2 rotis, a katori of toor dal (40 g dry), palak sabzi
- **Evening:** soya chunk chaat (40 g dry) with 20 g peanuts, onion, tomato and lemon, and an apple
- **Dinner:** paneer bhurji (100 g paneer) with rice (60 g dry)

### Eggetarian (about 2,230 kcal, 113 g protein)

- **Breakfast:** omelette of 3 whole eggs and 3 whites, 2 slices of brown bread
- **Mid-morning:** 250 ml toned milk and a banana
- **Lunch:** rajma (50 g dry) with rice (90 g dry) and a katori of curd
- **Evening:** a guava and 30 g roasted peanuts
- **Dinner:** 2 rotis, moong dal (40 g dry), gobi sabzi with 60 g paneer

### Non-vegetarian (about 2,200 kcal, 134 g protein)

- **Breakfast:** egg bhurji (2 whole eggs and 2 whites) with 1 roti, 250 ml toned milk and a banana
- **Lunch:** chicken curry (150 g boneless breast) with rice (120 g dry) and a katori of curd or raita
- **Evening:** a bowl of papaya and 15 g peanuts
- **Dinner:** fish curry (150 g rohu), 2 rotis, a small katori of masoor dal (30 g dry) and bhindi

### Vegan (about 2,180 kcal, 118 g protein)

- **Breakfast:** poha (60 g dry) with 30 g peanuts, 250 ml soy milk and a banana
- **Lunch:** 2 rotis, chole (60 g dry chickpeas) and a soya chunk sabzi (50 g dry)
- **Evening:** an orange and a tablespoon of pumpkin seeds
- **Dinner:** tofu palak (150 g tofu), moong dal (40 g dry) and rice (70 g dry)

## Cheap high-protein Indian foods

Some of the best protein sources are also the cheapest in any Indian market:

- **Soya chunks:** the most protein per rupee of almost anything, about 26 g per 50 g dry
- **Chana, rajma and lobia,** bought dry and pressure-cooked at home
- **Besan and sattu,** for chillas or a sattu drink with water, lemon and salt
- **Dals of every kind**
- **Peanuts,** roasted or as chikki in moderation
- **Eggs,** among the best value for eggetarians and non-vegetarians
- **Toned milk and home-set curd;** hang the curd in a cloth for a few hours to make thick, high-protein hung curd
- **Skimmed milk powder,** about 11 g of protein per 30 g, stirred into milk, curd or oats
- **Chicken curry cut and seasonal local fish**

## Making dal-roti-rice higher in protein

The classic thali is often carb-heavy with a thin, watery dal.
Small changes fix that without changing what you eat:

1. **Make the dal thicker.** Use 40-50 g of dry dal per person instead of 20-25 g.
2. **Shift the ratio.** Take a second katori of dal and a smaller mound of rice.
3. **Add a protein side to every meal:** curd or raita, a boiled egg, a piece of chicken or fish, or a bowl of sprouts chaat.
4. **Put protein into the sabzi:** paneer or tofu in palak, soya chunks in aloo-matar, egg in bhurji, chana in a dry sabzi.
5. **Upgrade the atta.** Mix in some besan or soya flour for rotis and parathas.
6. **Swap the breakfast.** Besan or moong dal chilla, egg bhurji or paneer-stuffed paratha with curd instead of plain poha, upma or white bread.
7. **Use milk and curd deliberately.** A glass of milk or a bowl of curd adds 7-8 g with no cooking.
8. **Watch the tadka.** A generous spoon of ghee or oil adds 100-120 kcal and almost no protein.

## A few practical habits

- Cook dals and pulses in bulk and refrigerate for 2-3 days.
- Soak and boil chana or rajma on the weekend for quick snacks.
- When eating out, pick tandoori, tikka, grilled fish, dal and roti over gravies swimming in cream and butter.
- At weddings and festivals, enjoy them; one day does not undo a week.
